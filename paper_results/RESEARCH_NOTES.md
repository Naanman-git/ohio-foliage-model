# Research on predicting autumn foliage

## Closest models to the Ohio project

| Study | What it contributes to the Ohio report |
|---|---|
| Archetti, Richardson, O’Keefe & Delpierre (2013), *PLOS ONE* | The closest match. The authors used long-term Harvard Forest observations for eight tree species and compared regressions with a cold degree day model weighted by day length. They modeled multiple observed stages of leaf coloration and leaf fall. Our simpler Ohio model uses daily minimum temperatures and one target, the 50% crossing. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0057373) |
| Delpierre et al. (2009), *Agricultural and Forest Meteorology* | A direct precedent for accumulating cold and day-length effects up to a threshold. It modeled interannual and spatial variation in leaf coloring for deciduous trees in France. Its fitted parameters should not be imported as Ohio constants. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.agrformet.2008.11.014) |
| Jolly, Nemani & Running (2005), *Global Change Biology* | The challenge packet cites this Growing Season Index. It uses minimum temperature, day length, and atmospheric moisture demand to estimate vegetation activity, giving a documented candidate for a future benchmark. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2486.2005.00930.x) |
| Meier & Bigler (2023), *Geoscientific Model Development* | Compared 21 process models with alternative calibration choices. It supports evaluating model structure and treating future shifts as uncertain. It includes extensions for seasonal conditions and spring phenology. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-16-7171-2023) |
| Spafford et al. (2025), *Agricultural and Forest Meteorology* | Tested transfer over time and at external sites, along a large Swiss elevation gradient. Its validation design is a precedent for future held-out Ohio sites. Our historical backtest tests later years at monitored locations. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.agrformet.2025.110685) |

## Drivers worth discussing

- **Weather beyond fall temperatures.** Xie et al. found that drought, frost, rain, and heat were associated with forest dormancy timing in New England. They also examined species-specific responses to weather stressors. A 2026 USA-NPN-based study found growing-season temperature was the leading association in the eastern U.S. and preseason precipitation was stronger in the west. This supports testing a single additional weather variable with enough Ohio observations. It does not supply an Ohio coefficient or establish that adding rain will improve the current backtest. [Xie et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1509991112) · [Xie et al. 2018](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0168192317306792) · [Eastern and western U.S. study, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.agrformet.2026.111190)
- **Spring and species.** Keenan & Richardson found a relationship between spring phenology and fall senescence in northeastern U.S. observations. A 29-year Illinois study covering 22 woody taxa found species-specific responses, and earlier spring timing was not among its strongest predictors of earlier coloration. These findings support documenting the mix of trees and checking spring data only where the same trees have usable spring and fall records. [Keenan & Richardson 2015](https://doi.org/10.1111/gcb.12890) · [Illinois long-term field study](https://doi.org/10.1002/ece3.71226)
- **Warming response.** Across more than 500,000 European observations, Liu et al. found stronger responses in warm than cold autumns, with nighttime warming playing a role. This supports describing our temperature perturbations as conditional scenarios with a simple response model. [Liu et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41558-020-0820-2)

## Independent data and validation

- **USA-NPN field observations** record the fraction of a monitored plant's potential canopy occupied by non-green leaves. This is the direct measurement underlying the Ohio threshold. With visits on different days, the crossing remains interval-censored. [Observation protocol](https://www.usanpn.org/files/reports/usa-npn_plant_and_animal_phenophase_definitions_v2.0.pdf)
- **Repeat photography** can give canopy-level measures. A New England field study matched red camera indices to autumn field stages across several species. PhenoCam v3 provides a documented archive, and candidate Ohio views need to be checked for deciduous forest coverage, years, and compatible targets. [Camera validation study](https://doi.org/10.1002/ecs2.2089) · [PhenoCam v3](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/17/6531/2025/)
- **MODIS MCD12Q2** provides annual satellite greenness and dormancy metrics at 500 m. A forest-pixel greendown date could serve as a separate landscape comparison. It measures a different event from a monitored tree crossing 50% non-green canopy. [NASA product guide](https://modis-land.gsfc.nasa.gov/pdf/MODIS_Land_Cover_Dynamics_MCD12Q2_C61_Product.pdf)

For the paper, use Delpierre et al. and Archetti et al. to motivate the equation, USA-NPN to define the observation, and Meier & Bigler to frame calibration and uncertainty. Keep numerical accuracy claims tied to the Ohio backtest. Any new driver should be tested against the same historical-median and current model baselines with the same holdout protocol.

## Additional sources for the species and rainfall experiments

- Archetti et al. compared tree species and multiple color and leaf-fall stages using the same forest field program. This is the precedent for analyzing species in the same site and year before pooling their offsets. [Full paper](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0057373)
- An eastern and central U.S. satellite study reported geographically varying associations between color onset, late-season temperatures and precipitation. Satellite peak onset and individual tree color thresholds refer to different observables. [NOAA repository copy](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/26072/noaa_26072_DS1.pdf)
- NOAA daily precipitation is from the same three stations as the core model. Quality-flagged observations are excluded, and each summer requires 88 of 92 valid daily values. [Download](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/services/data/v1?dataset=daily-summaries&stations=USW00004804,USW00053855,USC00335857&startDate=2018-03-01&endDate=2026-11-10&dataTypes=PRCP&includeAttributes=true&units=metric&format=csv)
- The user-provided challenge PDF defines the required deliverables. The user-provided reference pack documents the original modeling concept. The original notebook and Python prototype show the early synthetic workflow. The weather forecast images shared by the user informed the presentation of daily percentages and carry no foliage observations.
- [2026 source log](2026_news_validation.csv) records local reporting, forecasts, secondary reproductions of forestry maps, and season observations separately. The original archived observations and forecasts are retained in the project-root `foliage_evidence.csv`.
- Spring budbreak coverage uses USA-NPN Nature’s Notebook phases 371 and 373, queried for station IDs 859, 35743, 34167 and 57318 from January 2018 through June 2026. The local extract keeps site, plant, date and status. The coverage analysis only uses January–June in each year. [USA-NPN data access](https://www.usanpn.org/data/observational)
